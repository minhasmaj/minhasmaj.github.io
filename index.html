About

Dr. Minhas Majeed Khan is an independent scholar, author, and geopolitical policy analyst specialising in international relations theory, Pakistan's foreign policy, and the geopolitics of the Global South. Her scholarship is grounded in the conviction that power without moral authority is neither durable nor legitimate — a principle that runs through her academic writing, public commentary, and original theoretical work.

She holds a doctorate from the University of Peshawar and has held postdoctoral fellowships at the Institute of Strategic Studies Islamabad (ISSI) and the Institute for Global Engagement (IGE) in Washington D.C. Her public analysis appears across BBC, Voice of America, and leading Pakistani media. She serves on the IGE Advisory Council and has contributed to journals including Strategic Studies (ISSI) and Peshawar Islamicus.

Her intellectual formation draws on Islamic moral tradition, classical realist theory, and a civilisational perspective that consistently situates Pakistan within the broader currents of Global South geopolitics.

Divine Diplomacy
Divine Diplomacy: Post-9/11 Evangelical Influence on Pakistan–US Relations
Palgrave Macmillan  ·  2025
A rigorous examination of how evangelical religious networks shaped the strategic relationship between Pakistan and the United States in the two decades following 9/11. The study brings the religion-security nexus to the centre of foreign policy analysis, challenging realist accounts that marginalise the role of faith-based actors in interstate diplomacy.
Original Theory
Khan's Exhaustion Asymmetry
KEA  ·  An original geopolitical framework  ·  2026
Khan's Exhaustion Asymmetry (KEA) proposes that the outcome of asymmetric conflict is determined not by military superiority but by the differential rate at which each belligerent's civilisational condition deteriorates across four measurable dimensions. The actor whose condition erodes faster reaches a threshold of unsustainability — what KEA terms the exhaustion point — first. When both belligerents exhaust simultaneously, structural conditions emerge for a third actor to assume the mediating role. KEA extends and challenges Robert Pape's Escalation Trap, arguing that exhaustion is not a byproduct of escalation but a prior structural condition that predicts and shapes it.
